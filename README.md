## Where the plan list works against you

Two things in that table are worth understanding before you pick.

**The Pro lines invert the price-to-spec ratio.** JPPro-Light costs **¥24.99**, 67% more than JPHyper-Lite at ¥14.99, and gives you half the RAM (1 GB instead of 2 GB), 30% less traffic (700 GB instead of 1 TB), and a port running at roughly one eighth the speed. In Los Angeles the gap is wider: LAXPro-Light is ¥43.74 for 300 GB on a 500 Mbps port, while LAXLite-One is ¥8.88 for 1 TB on 5 Gbps. You are paying about five times as much for a fifth of the traffic.

What the Pro tiers presumably buy is routing. AkileCloud's plan notes mention "direct connection and route-tuned models" for the Japan series and describe the LAX Premium line as two-way optimised across all three Chinese carriers (Unicom 9929, China Mobile CMIN2, Telecom CN2 GIA, native IP). Those are real advantages if mainland latency is your problem. The catch is that the Japan notes don't say which model uses which route. Paying triple for routing you can't identify before checkout is a bad trade, so read the routing line on the specific plan page first.

**The 5 GB entry disk is a real ceiling.** LAXLite-One and SGLite-One both ship 5 GB. A control panel plus a couple of Docker images eats most of that, and you have no room for logs or a database. If you plan to run anything that writes to disk, the ¥7 step to LAXLite-Mini (10 GB disk, 2 GB RAM, 2 TB traffic) is the cheapest useful upgrade in the catalogue. If disk is your bottleneck rather than bandwidth, LAXPro-Light's 20 GB for ¥43.74 is the option, though you pay for it in traffic and port speed.

## Picking a region

**Los Angeles** is the value end. 5 Gbps ports on the Lite tier, the lowest entry price, and the largest traffic allowances per yuan. It suits proxies, download boxes, mirrors, and services aimed at a global audience. If your users are in mainland China, the plain LAXLite tiers run ordinary international routing, so look at the LAX Premium line instead and expect to start around ¥43.74.

**Japan** is the best all-rounder. JPHyper-Lite at ¥14.99 is the only plan under ¥20 that gives you 2 GB of RAM, and the Japan plan notes mention local streaming unlock, which matters if you want Japanese catalogue access.

**Singapore** is the thinnest of the four. The entry plan has a 500 Mbps port and 5 GB of disk for ¥10.42, only ¥1.54 less than LAXLite-One on a port ten times faster. It makes sense for small always-on services, bots and API endpoints where latency to Southeast Asia matters and throughput doesn't.

**Hong Kong** is the mainland-adjacent option, with 2.5 TB of traffic at 1 Gbps for ¥24.99. The recurring problem is stock: the Hong Kong MOLite series has been listed as sold out across the board, and HKLite-Mini is the one that stays orderable. Region-level availability means nothing here. Check that the individual plan shows stock.

## What it costs after the first invoice

Everything in the catalogue is quoted as a monthly CNY figure. There is no published annual discount rate to calculate against and no verified multi-year price lock, so the only honest way to model the second and third year is to multiply the monthly price by 12: ¥8.88 becomes ¥106.56 a year, ¥14.99 becomes ¥179.88.

Three things affect what you actually pay.

**Currency.** Paying in yuan keeps the price you see identical to the price you pay. Paying from a USD card adds a conversion spread on every invoice, and because the catalogue's dollar equivalents move with the exchange rate, the USD number quoted elsewhere is not a fixed price.

**Promotions.** AkileCloud's prices shift with campaigns, and plans move in and out of stock. A plan you bookmark at ¥14.99 may be re-priced or unavailable a month later. Budget providers in this segment compete on promotional pricing, not stable list pricing.

**Refunds.** I could not confirm a standing refund window for these plans, whether refunds are limited to first purchases, or whether promotional plans are excluded. That uncertainty is the strongest argument for starting with a single month rather than an annual prepay. One month of JPHyper-Lite costs ¥14.99. Losing that is cheaper than losing a year of something that turns out to route badly for your users.

## Who should buy somewhere else

AkileCloud is a budget shared-CPU provider, and the plan list reflects that.

If you need an SLA or you're moving production traffic, this isn't the tier for it. A LowEndTalk user quoted in a community roundup reported Geekbench 5 single-core around 1300 on a cheap Singapore plan, running two Discord bots and a light API without trouble, while also noting some CPU steal. That is a fair summary of what these boxes are good at: small, always-on, low-stakes workloads.

If you need more than 40 GB of disk, the catalogue tops out at LAXPro-Max's 40 GB. If you need a Windows licence bundled, or a specific number of extra IPv4 addresses, those aren't documented in the public plan list, so ask support before you pay rather than after.

And if your whole reason for buying is low latency into mainland China, be honest about the budget. The Lite tiers won't do it. The optimised routes start at ¥43.74, and at that price you should be comparing against other providers' premium China-optimised lines before committing.

The referral parameter travels with the link you arrive from and attaches at registration, so sign up through the same link you used to browse rather than opening the shop separately later.

## FAQ

**Does AkileCloud accept Alipay?** The entire catalogue is priced in yuan, which tells you CNY payment is the intended path. The exact wallet and card options appear at the payment step, and I could not confirm the full list.

**Can I change the IP later?** Not documented publicly. If a clean IP is critical to what you're deploying, ask support before ordering.

**What happens if I go over the traffic allowance?** The plan list doesn't publish an overage policy. If you expect to run near the cap, buy the next tier up instead of relying on how overages are handled.

**Can I install Windows?** Not confirmed for these plans. The KVM-based ones can technically run it, but licensing and ISO support need a direct answer from support.

**Is there a refund?** Unconfirmed. Pay monthly for the first cycle and treat that as your risk control.

## How to decide

Running only a proxy or a light service and price is the main criterion: **LAXLite-One at ¥8.88**, or SGLite-One at ¥10.42 if Southeast Asia latency matters more than port speed.

Running a panel, a couple of bots, or a small site that writes to disk: **JPHyper-Lite at ¥14.99**. It's the only sub-¥20 plan with 2 GB of RAM, and the disk is twice the entry size.

Serving mainland users and latency is the priority: skip the Lite tiers and price out the LAX Premium or HKPro routes, starting around ¥43.74.

Need more than 1 TB or multiple concurrent users: **LAXLite-Starter at ¥34.99** gives 2 cores, 2 GB RAM and 5 TB on a 5 Gbps port, which is the best spec-per-yuan in the catalogue.

Before you pay, confirm two things on the order page: that the specific plan shows stock, and what the routing note for that node actually says. Everything else in the table is arithmetic.
